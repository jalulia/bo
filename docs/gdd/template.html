DESKTOP BO A desktop pet starring Julia's real cats, drawn the way PF Magic drew Catz in 1996, living in rooms painted the way RollerCoaster Tycoon painted parks. Elevator pitch. Catz, except the cats are Bo and Bunny, the furniture is Julia's furniture, the backyard is her mother's backyard with her mother's dog in it, and nothing is a chore. No meter punishes you. Nothing dies. The only objective is that she looks at it and says "that's so Bo." Tone. Soothing, funny, specific. Her words, collected: "so cute and soothing"; "cats are superlanguine"; "intention in the physical geometry of the walk"; "snappy-stretchy"; "the comraderie comfort part"; "RCT-quality scenery." Core loop. 1. They live. A state machine picks what each cat does from weighted wants: nap, wander, groom, graze, watch the pigeon, start a fight. 2. You put things out. The toy box holds one of each thing per place: beds, chairs, plants, toys, treats, seeds and tools. 3. You touch. Pet (press and stroke), poke (tap a face, a leg, a tail), carry (press and drag), set her down in a bed, brush her, dress her, play the laser. 4. Time passes on the real clock. Dawn, day, dusk, night change the light. The garden grows while the app is closed. 5. A second cat changes the room. Bunny wallops Bo; they brawl; they nap together in the sun. Bond and tension drift. 6. Out back, Juno watches from her bed. Bunny sometimes joins her. Bo gives her the look. Win / lose. The player cannot win or lose. The builder can: a cat sinks into the floor, a leg goes stumpy, a thing needs explaining, Juno is terrifying. Those are the fail states, and the record of each one is in ROUNDS.LOG. Player role. The hand. You are the person they live with, which is the only reason they look up. Three places. Bo's Jungle (the room, a window into it), the Backyard (out the studio doors), My desktop (they roam the whole screen; Mac app only). How to play (PLAYPEN.EXE window). The window on the left is the habitat; the toy box on the right is the real toy box. Click a thing, then click in the window to put it down (small things go on the sill, the shelves, the table). Garden → a seed packet, then click a bed or a grass patch; the can waters, the trowel digs up. Press the hour buttons to pin the time of day. Casting. Bo (seal point & white Cornish Rex, 19, aloof). Bunny (all white, older, tinier, feisty). Juno (Mom's Saluki, on a bed). Five more rexes can be adopted after Bunny; each has a personality and a name from the hat.

BEHAVIOR.DAT - The state machine, attention, needs, beds, the dog

Each cat is a state machine. Every state is a method s_<name>() on Cat in world.js, run every tick; setState() resets per-state fields. From idle, pickActivity() draws from a weighted table that personality, needs, mood, the other cat and the things in the room all push on. BUSY states can't be interrupted by the world; NAPPING states recover energy.

Needs, moods, relationships
  • Needs 0–100: belly, energy, love, greens. They drift; nothing punishes you for letting them.
  • Moods 0–1: grump, cuddle, play. Petting lifts cuddle; the watering can and a bad plant lift grump; a fresh box lifts play.
  • Excitement rises with play and zoomies, decays at rest.
  • Relationship per pair: bond and tension. Shared sunshine cools tension (the comfort zone). Play-fights raise bond. "Love / hate / love."
  • Attention: every stimulus (laser, wand, toy, butterfly below z 34, leaf, squirrel, ground bird, the pigeon) carries an interest level per cat; it has to cross her threshold (aloof raises it) and survive her latency (Bo 22–70 ticks, Bunny 5–22) before she engages.

CLOSET.EXE - The toy box catalogue (one of each thing per place)

Every placeable thing is a row in BZ.ITEMS (items.js): a label, a hint, a hit box (r, h) and flags. The flags are the design: bed (cats nap on it), soft, box (climb in), chair, plant, snack, tool (lives on your cursor), seed, places (which toy box offers it). Icons are drawn by the engine.

Fixtures and surfaces (not in the box)
Fixtures (fixed: true, provided by the place, never dragged or saved): the two sill ledges, the daybed and coffee table (room), the felt cube, the daybed's second cushion and Juno herself (room); the steps, the potting-shelf perch, the tire swing, the Adirondack chair, Juno's bed (yard). syncFixtures() keeps the same objects across relayouts so a sitting cat isn't knocked off.

PLACES.MAP - Bo's Jungle · Backyard · My desktop
What's alive out back
  • Birds at the feeder, Northern Virginia regulars by season: cardinal (m/f), blue jay (bosses the tray), Carolina chickadee, tufted titmouse, goldfinch (yellow in summer), house finch, Carolina wren, downy woodpecker and white-breasted nuthatch (cling to the pole and trunk), mourning dove, robin (hops on the lawn), juncos in winter.   • The squirrel comes down the trunk every 1800–4400 ticks; 40% of the time it raids the feeder. Cats chatter and stalk; Juno boofs.
  • Butterflies (2 by day) visit grown flowers; cats bat at them only when they dip below z 34. Leaves let go and drift. Fireflies at night. The dapple shimmers; the sun pools drift with the hour.

BALLZ.DOC - Art direction: how a cat is drawn, how a room is painted
1 · The cats: Ballz

PF Magic's technique (Ballz 1994 → Dogz 1995 → Catz 1996), rebuilt from the reverse-engineering record in ballz-technique.json: a pet is a list of balls (shaded discs) and tapered lines between them, re-posed every frame in 3D, projected with a yaw-and-pitch camera, sorted far to near and drawn with a 1-px outline around the whole silhouette. No sprite sheets. Ours adds Cornish Rex proportions (egg head, huge ears, Roman nose, long legs, whip tail), marcel-wave speckle on the coat, IK forelegs, a 15-joint spring tail with a gesture layer, and the outline pass as one image-data step.

Bo at 2×: the drawn cat, and beside her the same frame with every ball center ringed, which is all the skeleton is. Body balls: hip, back, chest, belly; head: skull, muzzle, cheeks, ears; four legs of root → knee → paw; the tail chain. Hind legs: thigh 7.5, shank 10.5, hock 11 above the paw (shorter was "munchkin"). Ground-rest lift weighted by min(1, |roll|/35) so no cat sinks after a nap.

2 · The places: RCT rules
  • One light, upper right (the window; the sun). Top and right edges catch it; bottom and left edges shade. Contact shadows are rgba(20,14,6,0.28) ellipses.
  • Ramps of 5–8 steps, one per material; never an ad hoc color. Below are the live ramps from rex.js.
  • Ordered dither: rampAt(ramp, L, x, y) adds a 4×4 Bayer value to the light level before picking a step. Per-pixel shading goes through shade(ctx, pts, fn) (polygon scanline fill).
  • Big things are cached once per size (cached(key, w, h, ox, oy, paint)) and cost nothing per frame.
  • Specific over generic: these are her things, painted from the photo. If a thing is big enough to look at, draw it like a chair (shaded polygons, specific detail); the first easel and kick wheel were flat sketches and she said so.
  • Alive, quietly: clouds, curtains, dust, a pigeon; dapple, leaves, butterflies, birds, a squirrel. Nothing loud.

Input

Pointer events (mouse, pen, touch). A lifted finger means no pointer: the laser dot goes away, cats stop staring. Hit order: a cat's drawn balls (regions head / back / leg / tail), then Juno's, then items. Press + stroke = pet (purr, lean, ears); a quick tap on a face, leg or tail = poke; press + drag past the bbox = carry; drop over a free bed = place. The brush grooms on contact. A finger dragged on empty ground pans the camera when there is one.

Hosts

Mac app (/Applications/Desktop Bo.app): Electron; a habitat window plus a toy-box popover; roam mode over the desktop; state in ~/Library/Application Support/Desktop Bo/state.json. Only engine/ changes between rounds (an update zip swaps it, re-signs ad hoc, relaunches). Browser playpen: one published page, same engine inlined; phone layout with a bottom-sheet toy box and a camera; Add to Home Screen works. This document: the engine again, in an iframe, with the hour pinnable.

// world.js — Bo's look at Juno, entered from the scene when Bo wanders within 85·size of the dog
s_skeptic() {
  const d = this.skepAt; if (!d) { this.setState('idle'); return; }
  this.gait = null; this.anim = this.st > 30 && this.pers.sleepy > 50 ? 'sit' : 'stand';
  const H = d.screen && d.screen.head ? d.screen.head : [d.x, d.y - 20];
  this.turnToward(this.angleTo(d.x, d.y), 1); this.lookAt = [H[0], H[1]]; this.perk = 0.7; this.pose.earBack = 0.55;
  if (this.st === 10) this.tailGesture('flick'); if (this.st === 24 && ri(3) === 0) this.say('meow');
  if (this.st > (this.skepT || 90)) { this.pose.earBack = 0; this.mood.grump = Math.min(1, this.mood.grump + 0.05); this.setState('wander'); }
}
