CHAPTER / 01

Game

Two real cats and a real dog live in rooms painted from their owner's photographs. They act on their own state machines on the real clock; the player is the hand that pets, carries, feeds, plants and puts things out. There is nothing to win.

01Player role02Experience goals03Three places04Non-goals05Design decisions

Player role

The player is the person the animals live with, which is the only reason they look up. They have no authority over the animals: they can furnish, touch, feed, plant and wait. Everything else the cats decide. The audience is Julia first, her mother second (a touch-first version is open, chapter 08), and anyone with the browser link.

Genre
Desktop pet / ambient life simulation. Lineage: Catz and Dogz (PF Magic, 1995–96), Neko, Tamagotchi with the meters removed.
Platforms
macOS app (Electron: a habitat window, or the cats roaming the whole desktop); one published browser page with a phone layout; the embed in chapter 06.
Session shape
Always on. Minutes of attention, hours of background. Leaving costs nothing; the garden keeps growing while the app is closed.
Content
2 cats and 5 adoptable coats; 1 dog; 13 bird kinds, a squirrel, a pigeon, butterflies; 52 placeable things; 3 places; 36 cat states; 8 seeds.

Each place keeps its own set of things. Switching stashes one set and restores the other; the cats walk in through the doors or from the edge.

Non-goals

  • No win, lose, score, currency, unlock economy or progression. Adoptable coats unlock in a fixed order; that is content pacing.
  • No notifications, nagging or guilt. A cat left alone for a week is exactly as happy.
  • No character creator. The cast is Julia's animals; the five coats are the only extensibility.
  • No multiplayer, cloud sync or accounts. State is a local file.
  • No generic assets. A thing without a reference photo is not built.

Open decisions: the touch-first version for Julia's mother (device, who sets it up); whether Juno ever walks again; whether fixtures become obstacles. These are held in chapter 08.

First session

The window opens on the room at the current hour. Bo is asleep on the sill or walking somewhere; Juno is on the daybed. Nothing asks for anything. The first fifteen minutes should show the three things the game is: they live, you furnish, you touch.

Attention states

The first thing a new player tries is the laser. What happens next is the clearest demonstration of the personality model: the same stimulus produces a chase from Bunny and a stare from Bo, through one mechanism.

Behavior & character

Each cat is a state machine that reads a fixed personality and drifting needs. The dog is a smaller machine that never leaves her bed. Character is what the same machine does with different numbers.

Attention and naps

Attention. Every stimulus (laser, wand, a toy in motion, a butterfly below z 34, a falling leaf, the squirrel, a ground bird, the pigeon landing) carries an interest level per cat. The bid must cross her threshold (raised by aloof) and survive her latency (Bo 22–70 ticks, Bunny 5–22) before she enters engage. A BUSY state ignores bids. Chapter 02, FIG. 02 shows the two outcomes.

Naps. A nap has a style and a bed. Style comes from the personality's nap mix, re-weighted per bed: dangle needs an edge, burrow needs the blanket, clipped beds (pod, cube) hide the body. The bed is a weighted draw over everything with bed:

Relationships. One record per pair: bond and tension, 0–1, saved. Shared sunshine cools tension (the comfort zone). Play-fights raise bond. Bo and Juno have no record; Bo's skeptic is a fixed reaction with a grump cost.

Art & sound

Two techniques, one pixel grid. The animals are drawn the way PF Magic drew Catz: balls and tapered lines posed in 3D, depth-sorted, outlined. The rooms are painted the way RollerCoaster Tycoon painted parks: one light, discrete ramps, ordered dither, specific objects.

Character construction

A character is a list of balls (shaded discs) and tapered lines between them, re-posed every frame in 3D, projected with a yaw-and-pitch camera, sorted far to near, and drawn with a one-pixel outline around the whole silhouette. There are no sprite sheets. The technique is rebuilt from the reverse-engineering record in reference page A. Additions: Cornish Rex proportions (egg head, huge ears, Roman nose, long legs, whip tail), marcel-wave speckle on the coat, IK forelegs, a 15-joint spring tail with a gesture layer, the outline as one image-data pass.

Painting rules

  1. One light, upper right (the window; the sun). Top and right edges catch it; bottom and left edges shade. Contact shadows are rgba(20,14,6,0.28) ellipses.
  2. Ramps of 5–8 steps, one per material; never an ad hoc color (06.04).
  3. Ordered dither: rampAt(ramp, L, x, y) adds a 4×4 Bayer value to the light level before picking a step. Per-pixel shading goes through shade(ctx, pts, fn), a polygon scanline fill.
  4. Cache big things once per size (cached(key, w, h, ox, oy, paint)); they cost nothing per frame.
  5. Specific over generic: painted from the filed photo. If a thing is big enough to look at, it is drawn like a chair: shaded polygons, specific detail. The first easel and kick wheel were flat sketches and were sent back.
  6. Alive, quietly: clouds, curtains, dust, a pigeon; dapple, leaves, butterflies, birds, a squirrel. Nothing loud.

Scale and material

Grid
The canvas renders at half CSS resolution (PX 2) and is scaled up with no smoothing. A standing Bo at size M is about 50 × 40 source pixels.
Ramps
One per material, below, read live from rex.js. Shading picks a step by light level plus dither; texture stays inside the material.
Interface
The toy box uses the app's own pixel face (BoPixel) for titles and a system sans for everything else; it is not on the pixel grid.

Motion and sound

  • Languid by default ("superlanguine"): walks start slow and settle; the stop eases over the last steps.
  • Snappy-stretchy for play: the lunge and the crab hop come from her videos; the wiggle precedes the pounce.
  • Intention in the walk: head leads, gaze on the target, tail carries mood (gesture layer: flick, hook, S, wrap).
  • Tempo per cat: Bo 0.85, Bunny 1.18; everything she does scales.
  • Sound is a formant synth (04.06); no samples; off until enabled.
